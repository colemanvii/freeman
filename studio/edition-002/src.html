Gabe —

Edition 001 put three ideas in front of you. Three reactions were saved there: explore the globe, refine the series, park the jacket. I cannot verify who saved them, so these are provisional directions, not decisions attributed to you.

All three ideas are still here, in the order you saw them, each marked with what has changed. After them are three proposals that are new. They connect: a record of every building, a film series, and one house that publishes all of it.

The ambition is simple to say. I think Freeman can be the most compelling hospitality builder in the country, and that the way there is to document the work better than anyone else does, and to publish it with the care we build with.

It ends with three decisions and a ninety-day plan.

— Cole

    THE IDEA

    Every finished photograph we own gives the credit to someone else. The room belongs to the designer, the plate to the chef, the picture to the photographer. What the builder did is not in the frame.

    The Building Record puts it there. One record per project, made of three things: the drawings, the photographs, and a plain account of what was kept, what was replaced and what was built new.

    WHAT EXISTS TODAY

    More than a sketch. Built Record Study 001 was built on 13 November around Muchacho Decatur: six photographs, five record drawings traced from them, and a short account. The sheet above is that study’s first drawing, running here.

    It lives on a branch, is not linked from any page of freemangc.com, and is marked not to be indexed.

    WHAT IS MISSING

    The real thing. No architect’s drawing of any Freeman project is on file. No dated construction photograph is filed to a project. Scope, dates, floor area and the project team are not written down anywhere I can cite.

    That is why construction is a reserved state on the sheet above, and why the drawings say “traced” on them. The study invents nothing. When the real material arrives, it replaces the tracing.

    How a record gets made

    THE PROTOCOL / FOUR HABITS
    1. AT PRECONSTRUCTIONAsk for the drawings

      Plan, elevations, sections, and written permission to redraw them for publication. Asked once, at the start, as part of how we work.

    2. ON SITEOne position, kept

      One safe, repeatable camera position per room. One person returns to it at each meaningful change, above all before work is closed up.

    3. AT CLOSEOUTWrite it down

      Kept, replaced, new. Dates, area, team. One page, checked by the superintendent who ran the job.

    4. AFTER OPENINGGo back

      The finished photographs from the same positions, then again a year on. The record keeps growing after we leave.

    Commercial rationale

    WHY THIS EARNS ITS COST
    1. WHO IT IS FOR

      The owner, operator or architect choosing a builder for a design-led hospitality project. They are comparing portfolios that all show finished rooms.

    2. WHAT IT WINS

      It is the proposal. For the next pursuit we send the record of the closest job, and it shows how we read drawings and handle an existing building. Designers get their work documented properly, which is a reason to bring us in again.

    3. WHAT IT COSTS

      Mostly habit. A request at the start, a camera position on site, a page at closeout. The assembly time per record is real and still to be measured on the first one.

    4. HOW WE WOULD KNOW

      Records are asked for by name. Partners link to theirs. A pursuit is won where the record was the thing we sent.

    Built for Generations

    A recurring original documentary series

    Hospitality is the moment a guest walks in. Freeman’s work is everything before it.THE PREMISE / FROM THE PERSPECTIVE TREATMENT, 12 NOVEMBER 2026

      WHAT CHANGED

      On a Mission was about building the company, told by you, in ten episodes. I think the refinement is to turn the camera around.

      Built for Generations is about the places and the people who make them. You are in it, as the person who reads the room and makes the call, and you never have to explain Freeman to a lens. The name is already ours: it is the line under the signature and the name of the Instagram account.

      WHY A SERIES

      One brand film says what a company wants to be. A series that keeps returning shows what it is. Each film is made on a real job, with a real partner, and goes out when their place opens.

      The opening sequence drawn for Episode 001 in chapter 02 still stands as the way the pilot begins.

      The rules

      SIX, AND THEY DO NOT BEND
      1. Nothing is staged.We may ask someone to wait. We never ask anyone to do it again.
      2. The sound was recorded there.The room, the tools, the weather. Music only if it was playing.
      3. Real speed, hard cuts.A cut lands when the action is finished, not on a beat.
      4. Text states facts.A place, a true date, a name and a trade. One label in, one mark out.
      5. Everyone is credited.Operators, designers, photographers, and each tradesperson who appears, with their consent.
      6. Safety is in the picture.Correct protection in frame, under the superintendent’s authority, always.

      A pilot, then a season

      SIX FORMATS / SUBJECTS DECIDED BY WHAT IS LIVE
      1. 001Another DayThe pilot. A day with you, from the dark office to a full room. Storyboard in chapter 02.
      2. 002A TuesdayOne ordinary working day on one site, gate to lockup.
      3. 003The DecisionOne decision, from the field condition to the finished detail. The designer approves the cut.
      4. 004TradesOne tradesperson, one task, in their own words, named on screen.
      5. 005The Night BeforeThe last hours before an opening. The clock is the plot.
      6. 006ReturnA room we built, in service, a year on. What wore in and what held.
      PREP001·002003·004005·006··

      Only the pilot is proposed for commissioning. Films 002 to 006 are the season it would lead to, at roughly one every two months, and they are decided after the pilot is watched. The formats come from the Perspective treatment written on 12 November. Which job each film is made on depends on what is under construction and on the owner’s permission, so no project is named here.

      Commercial rationale

      WHY THIS EARNS ITS COST
      1. WHO IT IS FOR

        Owners and developers before the first meeting. Operators on the week they open. The trades and the people we want to hire.

      2. WHAT IT WINS

        People hire builders they feel they know. A film made with an operator goes out to that operator’s audience when they open, with our name on the work. Trades who are credited share it.

      3. WHAT IT COSTS

        A filmmaker’s fee, which is not known because nobody has been asked. About a day of your time for each film. Owner approvals, which set the release dates.

      4. HOW WE WOULD KNOW

        A restaurateur forwards a film to an architect without a word of explanation. An operator asks for the file. An enquiry names a film.

      Tristan has not been asked. There is no quote, no agreement and no schedule behind this chapter. The first step is one conversation about one pilot.

      The House

      Freeman as a publishing house: photographs, records, films, print and collaborations in one catalogue

      THE IDEA

      Freeman already publishes. There is a photographs page, a set of project records, motion studies, a signature, an eagle. What is missing is the decision to treat them as one body of work.

      A publishing house is that decision. Everything that leaves the building gets a place in one catalogue, is made to one standard, and is signed the same way.

      ONE NAME

      No new brand. The work is signed Freeman, and the line under it is Built for Generations. The catalogue numbers are a filing system, not a product range.

        Commercial rationale

        WHY THIS EARNS ITS COST
        1. WHO IT IS FOR

          A few hundred people: architects, restaurateurs, hotel operators, developers and designers who decide where the next important place gets built, and by whom.

        2. WHAT IT WINS

          One coherent body of work in place of scattered posts. A printed record stays on a desk long after a link is closed. Work made with partners reaches their audience as well as ours.

        3. WHAT IT COSTS

          Editing time, which is mine. A print run, priced only after a proof. The discipline to publish less when the work is not ready.

        4. HOW WE WOULD KNOW

          Every published piece has a number, a date and a credit. Partners ask for copies. Introductions arrive that can be traced to something we made.
