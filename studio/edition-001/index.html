01

The Globe

An animated Freeman identity study

THE IDEA

Here’s what I’m imagining. We take the globe mark and let it live on the homepage. The signature stays exactly where it is and doesn’t move. Behind it, the world turns.

It should still look like a flat, printed insignia from another century. It just happens to be rotating, slowly, about once every thirteen seconds.

WHY IT COULD MATTER

Nobody else building restaurants in Georgia has a mark that does this. It’s old-world and very precise at the same time, which is how I think about us.

And it travels. The end card of a film. A jobsite sign. The back of a jacket. The point isn’t animation. It’s identity.

HOW IT’S MADE

This isn’t a video or a spinning picture. The meridians and parallels are projected from a real sphere and drawn, pixel by pixel, onto the same grid as the script. That’s why the lines stay crisp and the edges foreshorten the way a globe should.

A few kilobytes of code, nothing to load. It holds still for anyone whose device asks for reduced motion. The script is the production 160 × 61 bitmap, untouched. The globe’s line count and weight are set for this study and still need matching against the insignia artwork.

02

On a Mission

A documentary series about building the company

THE IDEA

We bring in a filmmaker, Tristan, to follow you and the company for a year. About one film a month, ten episodes, on YouTube.

Working title: Freeman — On a Mission. Possibly with Built for Generations underneath it.

The inspiration is how George Heaton documented building Represent: the journey of the company treated as the interesting thing. I want the philosophy, not the look. Our version belongs to hospitality construction and to Cartersville.

WHY IT COULD MATTER

People hire builders they feel they know. Ten honest films would let an owner, an architect or a future hire spend an hour with us before we ever meet.

It shouldn’t be ten advertisements. It should be a record of what building this business was actually like, including the hard parts.

HOW IT LOOKS

Observational, patient, precise. The camera watches the work instead of performing for it.

USE

  • Strong architectural compositions
  • Natural and practical light
  • Unforced portraits
  • Environmental sound
  • Hands, tools, drawings, materials
  • Real conversations
  • Early mornings against finished rooms

AVOID

  • Motivational music
  • Fake candid moments
  • Drone shots for their own sake
  • Corporate interview setups
  • Aggressive transitions
  • Inspirational speeches
  • Staged toughness

Twelve months, ten releases. One month up front to prepare and shoot the first film, and one open month mid-year for when a job or a holiday gets in the way.

A month

PROPOSED RHYTHM
  1. WEEK 1Visit

    Tristan with us for about two days. Field footage on whatever is live, plus one sit-down conversation.

  2. WEEK 2Edit

    He cuts. I send over drawings, dates and names so the facts are right.

  3. WEEK 3Review

    You and I watch it once and give one round of notes. Partners see anything that shows their project.

  4. WEEK 4Release

    Finish and publish. We pick next month’s subject from what’s actually happening.

Scope

INDICATIVE / NOTHING PRICED

WHAT I’M ASSUMING

  • Ten episodes over twelve months
  • One production visit a month, roughly two days
  • One recorded conversation with you per visit
  • Films of around eight to twelve minutes
  • One round of review per episode
  • A few short cuts from each film for social

STILL TO PRICE OR NEGOTIATE

  • Whether Tristan is interested and available at all
  • His fee: per episode, per day, or for the year
  • Whether editing, colour and sound are included
  • Music: licensed, original, or mostly none
  • Travel for sites outside the area
  • Who owns the raw footage and how we can reuse it
  • Permission from owners and partners to film their projects

Tristan hasn’t been asked. Nothing on this page is a quote, an agreement or his opinion. The first step would simply be a conversation about Episode 001.

03

Freeman × Represent

An exploratory clothing and brand collaboration

THE IDEA

I think there’s something here. Represent makes clothes and cares a lot about identity. We’re an actual construction company with people who build things. I’d like to find out whether there’s an honest meeting point.

The product I keep coming back to is one heavyweight work jacket. Working line: Workwear for people who build.

This is speculative. There is no partnership, endorsement or agreement with Represent, and they haven’t seen any of it.

PRODUCT STUDIES / DRAWN, NOT PHOTOGRAPHED

COLORWAY

The jacket

WORKING SPEC / ALL OF IT OPEN
SHELL
Washed heavyweight cotton canvas, the kind that takes a few years to break in.
CUT
Generous through the shoulder, straight to the hip. Room for a layer, not oversized for effect.
HARDWARE
Five metal tack buttons. One chest pocket, two lower pockets you can get a gloved hand into.
MARKS
Front: the script, small, on the chest. Back: the globe insignia, about the width of a hand span and a half. That’s it.
COLOURS
Natural washed canvas, and a dark umber. Red thread on both.
THE TEST
It has to be worth owning with the embroidery taken off. And one of our superintendents has to be willing to wear it on site.

BEYOND CLOTHING

We build physical places. Represent makes retail and cultural spaces as part of what it does. If a jacket ever led to a real relationship, could there one day be a room we build together?

That’s a question to look into, not an opportunity that exists. I haven’t seen anything suggesting they’re looking for a builder in the States. The jacket has to earn the conversation first.

A possible path

FOUR STAGES / NO DATES YET
  1. STAGE 1Direction

    Finish the visual and product direction until it’s something we’d be proud to show.

  2. STAGE 2Prototype

    Make one real jacket. Wear it for a month.

  3. STAGE 3Introduction

    Find a credible way in. We don’t have one yet.

  4. STAGE 4Conversation

    See whether there’s creative or commercial interest on their side. If not, we still have a great jacket.

QUESTIONS FOR GABE

  1. Does this feel authentic to Freeman?
  2. Would you wear this jacket?
  3. Is there a stronger product idea?
  4. Would exploring a collaboration like this be worthwhile?
  5. Who could help us get an introduction?
