An investigation into the typographic identity of Freeman General Contracting.

For two years Monaco has given Freeman a voice that sounds like a record: exact, quiet, unsentimental. The question isn’t which typeface is more beautiful. It’s what Freeman needs to say that a record can’t, and whether the record should go on doing the talking.

01

What we have

The site is a set of quiet, fixed compositions on Paper. One face does every job: the navigation, the labels, a 33-pixel statement, four paragraphs of philosophy, and the name of every project. Hierarchy comes only from size, capitals and the orange. There are no weights, no italics and no second voice.

I read the production source (styles.css, app.js, records.js on release/approved-v5-2026-10-18, which freemangc.com serves byte-for-byte today) and measured every composition at 1440 × 900 and 390 × 844. The plates in this study are rebuilt from those measurements. Every direction keeps the same copy, photographs, colour, rules and positions, so the only thing that changes is the type.

What Monaco gives Freeman

  1. Precision without coldness. Susan Kare and Kris Holmes drew Monaco for the 1984 Macintosh. It has a humanist hand, with open counters and a round a, so it reads as exact without ever reading as industrial.
  2. The record. Small uppercase labels on hairlines make every page read like a drawing set or a specification. This is the site’s most original idea, and it is a typographic one.
  3. A rhyme with the signature. The script is a 160 × 61 bitmap, and Monaco began as a bitmap too. The hand and the machine come from the same moment on the same screen.
  4. Humility. A builder speaking in a plain, workmanlike face leaves the talking to the photographs.

Where it limits us

  1. Most of the people we want to impress never see it. Monaco is an Apple system font and can’t be licensed for the web. On Windows the desktop stack falls to Courier New, and Windows is where architects work, because Revit runs only there. Android phones get DejaVu.
  2. One voice for every job. “× CLOSE” and “Buildings outlive the people who build them” come out in the same voice, at nearly the same weight.
  3. Essays in a terminal. At essay length a monospace sets English wide and evenly grey, with every i as wide as an m. The Company page reads like a readme.
  4. No bold and no italic. The bold FREEMAN GC is synthesised by the browser, because Monaco has no bold. There is no italic for place names or the titles of works.
  5. The signal has moved. In 2020, uppercase mono labels read as a specification sheet. By 2026 they are the house style of software, AI and crypto launches, and the same marks now read as “tech.”

Fixed in every direction

Paper and the three palettes · the orange · hairline rules · the pixel signature and the eagle · the four-corner composition · small uppercase navigation in the same positions · photography and crops · all copy. The study only redraws type. It isn’t a redesign.

05

Three finalists

1

F · Two Voices

Caslon voice · monospaced record

It is the only direction that adds something Freeman lacks without taking away anything it already has. The site keeps its record exactly, and gains a voice that can say “built for generations” and be believed.

2

A · The Instrument

Diatype Mono + Semi-Mono

The best answer if Freeman decides it is a record and nothing more: a single voice, made properly, licensed, and the same on every device. It is safe and better than today. It also says less than F.

3

C · American Gothic

Franklin lineage, weight as voice

The boldest credible idea, and the only one that is unmistakably a builder. Made quieter (ATF Franklin Gothic in its lighter weights, heavy capitals used once per page) it could be extraordinary. As drawn here it shouts at the signature.

Not taken forward: B is too anonymous. D is beautiful but says spa. E is a magazine, and it gives up the record.

06

Recommendation

Freeman does two things, and its typography should say so.

It keeps a record: places, clients, credits, photographs and the plain order of the work. And it makes a promise, that what it builds will outlast the people who built it. For two years Monaco has been asked to do both. It is superb at the first and unconvincing at the second.

The recommendation is to stop asking one face to do both jobs.

The record stays monospaced. That means the same Monaco lineage the site already uses on phones: Menlo on Apple devices, and the self-hosted Freeman Mono everywhere else, so Windows stops seeing Courier New. Every label, number, credit and caption, the navigation and the four corners keep their size, capitals and spacing. Squint and it is the same site. The homepage doesn’t change at all, because there the signature is the voice.

The voice becomes Caslon. That isn’t the fashionable serif; it’s nearly the opposite of one. Caslon is the type of American printing from the colonies to the founding, and it has been in continuous use for three hundred years. It is what “Built for Generations” looks like set in type. It is warm without decoration and literate without preciousness. It sits naturally beside a restaurant menu, an architect’s monograph and a hand-painted sign. It belongs with the eagle and the idea of inheritance, and it needs no ornament.

Why not a better grotesk? A grotesk would make Freeman look like its architects. A serif-only system would make it look like its clients. Two voices let Freeman look like itself: the builder who keeps impeccable records and believes in what it builds.

In ten years: Caslon hasn’t dated in three hundred, and the mono layer is already Freeman’s own. To a restaurateur, it says taste, literacy and restraint. To an architect, it says a firm that knows the difference between a label and a sentence.

Rules of the system

  1. The record speaks mono. Navigation, labels, numbers, credits, captions, controls and metadata, set in caps at 9–11 px with today’s tracking.
  2. The voice speaks Caslon. Statements, editorial text, project names. Always sentence case. Never capitals, never letterspaced.
  3. Italic has one job: places, and the titles of works.
  4. Caslon never below 13 px. Under that size the mono speaks.
  5. Never mix voices inside a phrase. They meet across a rule, never within a line of text.
  6. No ornament. No swashes, small-cap flourishes or long s. The signature and the eagle remain the only marks.

Now · no cost

Libre Caslon Text + Display (OFL), self-hosted, with the record on the existing Menlo → Freeman Mono stack, exactly as rendered in this study. That fixes the Courier New problem on desktop the same day.

Upgrade · before any launch moment

Founders Caslon (Klim): a faithful Caslon cut in separate optical sizes from text to poster, and better drawn than Libre Caslon at display sizes. Request Klim trials and re-run this study with them before licensing. If the record should be licensed too, test Founders Grotesk Mono and Pitch against the Monaco lineage.

Before shipping

A Windows and Android pass. Caslon’s old-style figures in prose only, with tabular lining figures in the mono. A 9 px check on non-retina screens. A Contact and Map pass, which this study doesn’t cover. And a decision on Company’s Services and People tabs.

Rendering, licences, limits

  • Every plate shows a real face, or says it doesn’t. Each direction measures its own fonts in your browser and puts a warning in its header if a face is missing. No substitute is presented as the face it replaces.
  • A · ABC Diatype Mono is Dinamo’s trial (Regular only), installed on this Mac and loaded with local(). It isn’t embedded or distributed, and it won’t render on another machine. The trial licence covers evaluation, not publication.
  • B · Neue Haas Grotesk Text is installed on this Mac (55, 65, 75) and loaded with local(). The licence of that install isn’t verified. The Display cut is missing, so the large sizes are an approximation of the proper optical size.
  • C, E, F use OFL fonts from Google Fonts: Libre Franklin, Source Serif 4, Libre Caslon. They render on any device. F’s record layer is production’s own stack, including the self-hosted DejaVu Sans Mono (free licence) already in /assets/fonts.
  • D · Optima is the Apple system copy, so it renders only on Apple devices. Publishing requires a Linotype web licence.
  • Not rendered: Söhne, Founders Grotesk, Founders Caslon, Suisse Int’l, Untitled, GT America, Favorit, Pitch. No trials were downloaded and no commercial fonts were acquired. Their assessments rest on the foundries’ published specimens and on long familiarity, not on these plates.
  • The register plate redraws the register column of PROJECTS. The map base is replaced by the project photograph from the former index layout, so type rather than cartography is being judged. Contact, Map and Access are outside this study.
  • Production is untouched. This study lives only in v5/studies/typography-001/ on study/typography-001, which has no upstream and hasn’t been pushed. No generator was run.
