# Freeman Site Content Migration Mandate

## Status

Active — October 4, 2026.

The new Freeman visual system is not a microsite or a reduced concept site. It is the design language for the real Freeman General Contractors website.

## Requirement

The final site must preserve and reorganize the substantive content from the previous Freeman site while presenting it through the new Freeman design doctrine.

Do not achieve visual purity by deleting useful business information.

## Preserve

Migrate and verify the useful existing material, including where applicable:

- company positioning and history
- service descriptions
- completed projects
- project names and locations
- project imagery
- team / founder information
- FAQs
- contact information
- relevant testimonials or proof
- useful SEO-facing copy
- hospitality, commercial, healthcare, and residential capability
- any factual project details that support credibility

## Transform, Do Not Paste

Legacy copy should be edited into the new system.

The goal is not to reproduce the old page structure. The goal is to retain the underlying facts and meaning while making them feel native to the new Freeman identity.

Prefer:

- shorter, stronger passages
- clear labels
- project records
- captions
- field evidence
- concise factual metadata
- authored editorial framing

Avoid:

- generic marketing filler
- duplicated claims
- unnecessary adjectives
- long undifferentiated text walls
- preserving old layout conventions merely because the copy came from them

## Site Architecture Principle

The new Freeman site should feel visually restrained while being informationally complete.

Quiet interface. Rich content.

A visitor should be able to understand:

1. What Freeman builds.
2. Where Freeman works.
3. Why Freeman is distinctive.
4. What Freeman has actually completed.
5. How Freeman works.
6. Who is responsible.
7. How to start a conversation.

## Relationship to the Design Doctrine

This mandate is subordinate to `DESIGN-DOCTRINE.md`.

When content density creates tension with the visual system, solve the information architecture rather than abandoning the visual doctrine.

Do not revert to generic web patterns simply to fit more copy.
