# Study 05 / Alder — visual review at 1440×900

Reviewed 14 October 2026 against commit `2a1fda3`. Captures in `1440x900/`.
Findings only; nothing below is implemented yet.

## Five largest opportunities, in order of impact

### 1. Orange has stopped being a pointer
Study 04 used orange for six tiny numerals. Study 05 uses it for every reference code, chapter number, arrow, status square, selected bar, gate line and row marker. Decisions shows about a dozen orange marks and Schedule a similar number, so nothing on either screen stands out.
**Opportunity:** at most two orange marks per screen: the chapter numeral and the one current selection, such as the chosen gate, topic or allowance. Everything else goes to ink or gray.

### 2. Internal reference codes make the proposal read like a database
`B-01`, `S-07`, `A-02`, `M-21`, `C-01` and `D-02` appear in nearly every row, alongside many uppercase micro-labels (`ACCOUNTABLE`, `NEXT`, `WKS`, `GATES`, `TO VERIFY`). The cross-links themselves are valuable, but the codes read as software, not as a builder writing to a founder. Dense hairlines add to the effect: Budget has roughly 25 rules on one screen.
**Opportunity:** name links in plain words ("the kitchen equipment boundary", "week 8 start gate") and keep the codes in the data and the printed record only. Cut the rules and caption labels by about half, and drop the rule under every small heading.

### 3. The opening has the right content but no dominant line
- The left column stacks seven elements of similar weight. "Alder" at 36px is modest on a widescreen display, and the thesis (19px) competes with it.
- The opening sits centered in empty space above and below while the left column itself is crowded.
- The contents are still six equal tiles, so Study 04's main critique (no editorial recommendation) is not yet answered. The notes ("Five exclusions listed", "Two unknowns lead") are counts rather than guidance.
- The only orange flag on the opening ("Internal: not ready for issue") draws the eye to the weakest chapter.

**Opportunity:** give the client name and thesis one clear, generous moment; let the two commercial lines read as a confident statement; make the reading path visible (the brief points to Schedule, Budget and Decisions); and move internal status out of the client's first view.

### 4. Relevant Work reads as an empty form
- Two large crossed placeholder frames and four "Not yet documented. Record needed…" rows repeat across all three topics. In a conference room this looks unfinished.
- The one substantive passage, "Relevance to Alder", sits fifth and last.
- "Evidence before anecdote." describes Freeman's method rather than Alder's question.

**Opportunity, without inventing any facts:** show one topic as a finished composition specimen, with quiet plate fields and captions in their final typographic form. State the verification requirement once rather than four times, and lead with the Alder-specific action.

### 5. Precision faults in the two strongest charts
- **Schedule axis:** week labels mark the start of a week, but gate markers mark the end, so the "8" label sits one week left of the W8 gate.
- **Schedule labels:** gate labels collide with the vertical selection line.
- **Schedule gate header:** the selected-gate title floats mid-row, and the three condition columns are ragged.
- **Budget amounts:** the three allowance amounts are not right-aligned with the amount column and sit on a different baseline.
- **Budget hierarchy:** the $1,500,000 total doesn't own the screen. The 28px rail heading carries equal weight, and the register competes with two flanking columns.

**Opportunity:** fix the alignment faults and make the number and the chart the unmistakable center of each screen.

## Smaller notes
- The "question answered" line under each chapter label duplicates the headline's job; one of them can go.
- People & Scope leaves its lower third empty while the delivery stages wrap to four or five lines in narrow columns.
- Decisions packs three link lines under each decision title, which makes the rows look like tickets.
